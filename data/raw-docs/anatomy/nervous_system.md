# The Nervous System

## Overview

The nervous system is the body's communication and control network. It processes sensory information, coordinates movement, regulates body functions, and enables thought, emotion, and memory. It consists of the central nervous system (CNS) and the peripheral nervous system (PNS).

## Central Nervous System

### The Brain

The brain is the most complex organ in the human body, weighing approximately 1.4 kg (3 lbs) and containing roughly 86 billion neurons.

#### Cerebrum

The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain, divided into two hemispheres (left and right) connected by the corpus callosum. The outer layer is the cerebral cortex — a folded sheet of grey matter responsible for:
- Conscious thought and decision-making
- Language and speech
- Memory and learning
- Voluntary movement
- Sensory processing (touch, vision, hearing, taste, smell)

The cerebrum is divided into four lobes:
- **Frontal lobe**: Planning, reasoning, voluntary movement, personality, and speech production (Broca's area).
- **Parietal lobe**: Sensory integration, spatial awareness, and touch processing.
- **Temporal lobe**: Hearing, memory, and language comprehension (Wernicke's area).
- **Occipital lobe**: Visual processing.

#### Cerebellum

Located at the back of the brain beneath the cerebrum, the cerebellum coordinates voluntary movement, balance, and fine motor control. It receives input from sensory systems and the spinal cord to regulate motor movements.

#### Brain Stem

The brain stem connects the brain to the spinal cord and controls automatic vital functions:
- **Midbrain**: Visual and auditory reflexes.
- **Pons**: Relays signals between the cerebellum and cerebrum; involved in sleep, respiration, and swallowing.
- **Medulla oblongata**: Controls breathing, heart rate, and blood pressure. Damage to the medulla is life-threatening.

#### Thalamus

The thalamus acts as a relay station for sensory and motor signals to the cerebral cortex, except for smell. Nearly all sensory information passes through the thalamus.

#### Hypothalamus

Located beneath the thalamus, the hypothalamus regulates:
- Body temperature
- Hunger and thirst
- Sleep and wake cycles (circadian rhythm)
- Hormone release via the pituitary gland

#### Limbic System

The limbic system is involved in emotion, memory, and motivation. Key structures include the amygdala (emotional responses including fear) and hippocampus (memory formation and spatial navigation).

### The Spinal Cord

The spinal cord is a cylindrical bundle of nerve fibres extending from the medulla oblongata through the vertebral column. It is approximately 45 cm (18 inches) long.

Functions:
- Transmits motor commands from the brain to muscles.
- Relays sensory information from the body to the brain.
- Mediates reflexes independently of the brain.

The spinal cord is protected by the vertebral column (spine) and surrounded by cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). It is divided into cervical (neck), thoracic (chest), lumbar (lower back), and sacral (pelvis) segments.

## Peripheral Nervous System

The PNS consists of all nerves outside the brain and spinal cord.

### Somatic Nervous System

Controls voluntary movement of skeletal muscles and carries sensory information to the CNS.

### Autonomic Nervous System

Controls involuntary functions and has two divisions:
- **Sympathetic**: "Fight or flight" — increases heart rate, dilates pupils, redirects blood to muscles.
- **Parasympathetic**: "Rest and digest" — slows heart rate, stimulates digestion, conserves energy.

## Neurons

Neurons are the basic functional units of the nervous system. Each neuron has:
- **Cell body (soma)**: Contains the nucleus.
- **Dendrites**: Receive signals from other neurons.
- **Axon**: Transmits signals away from the cell body.
- **Myelin sheath**: Fatty insulation around the axon that speeds signal transmission.

Neurons communicate via electrochemical signals called action potentials across synapses using neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin, glutamate, and GABA.

## Cerebrospinal Fluid

CSF is a clear fluid surrounding the brain and spinal cord. It cushions the brain, removes waste, and provides nutrients. It is produced by the choroid plexus in the brain's ventricles.

## Headaches and the Brain

Headaches are among the most common neurological complaints. Primary headaches (tension, migraine, cluster) do not indicate underlying disease. Migraine involves changes in brain activity and blood flow — often preceded by an aura (visual disturbances, tingling). Secondary headaches result from another condition and may require medical evaluation.

The brain itself has no pain receptors; headache pain arises from surrounding structures including blood vessels, meninges, and muscles.

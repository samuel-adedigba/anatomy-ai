# Foundations of Human Anatomy

## The Human Body Systems

The human body is organised into eleven major organ systems that work together to maintain life:

1. **Integumentary system**: Skin, hair, nails — protection barrier.
2. **Skeletal system**: Bones, cartilage, ligaments — support and protection.
3. **Muscular system**: Skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscles — movement.
4. **Nervous system**: Brain, spinal cord, nerves — communication and control.
5. **Endocrine system**: Glands producing hormones — chemical regulation.
6. **Cardiovascular system**: Heart and blood vessels — circulation.
7. **Lymphatic system**: Lymph nodes and vessels, spleen — immunity and fluid balance.
8. **Respiratory system**: Lungs and airways — gas exchange.
9. **Digestive system**: GI tract and accessory organs — nutrient processing.
10. **Urinary system**: Kidneys, bladder, ureters — waste filtration.
11. **Reproductive system**: Gonads, ducts, external genitalia — reproduction.

## Levels of Biological Organisation

From smallest to largest:
- **Chemical level**: Atoms and molecules (water, proteins, DNA, lipids).
- **Cellular level**: The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
- **Tissue level**: Groups of similar cells performing a shared function.
- **Organ level**: Two or more tissue types forming a structure with a specific function.
- **Organ system level**: Multiple organs cooperating toward a broader function.
- **Organism level**: All systems working together to maintain a living human being.

## Types of Body Tissue

- **Epithelial tissue**: Covers body surfaces, lines cavities, forms glands. Functions as protection, secretion, absorption, filtration.
- **Connective tissue**: Most abundant type. Includes bone, cartilage, blood, fat, and fibrous tissue. Supports, binds, and separates other tissues.
- **Muscle tissue**: Contracts to produce movement (skeletal, cardiac, smooth).
- **Nervous tissue**: Neurons and supporting glial cells; transmits electrical signals.

## Anatomical Position and Planes

The standard anatomical position: body erect, facing forward, palms facing anteriorly.

**Anatomical planes**:
- **Sagittal plane**: Divides body into left and right.
- **Frontal (coronal) plane**: Divides body into front (anterior) and back (posterior).
- **Transverse (horizontal) plane**: Divides body into upper (superior) and lower (inferior).

**Directional terms**:
- Anterior/Posterior: Front/Back
- Superior/Inferior: Upper/Lower
- Medial/Lateral: Toward midline/Away from midline
- Proximal/Distal: Closer to/Further from point of attachment
- Superficial/Deep: Near surface/Away from surface

## Body Cavities

- **Dorsal cavity**: Contains cranial cavity (brain) and vertebral canal (spinal cord).
- **Ventral cavity**: Contains thoracic cavity (heart, lungs), abdominal cavity (digestive organs), pelvic cavity (bladder, reproductive organs).

## Homeostasis

Homeostasis is the body's ability to maintain a stable internal environment despite external changes. Examples include:
- Body temperature regulation (~37°C / 98.6°F) by the hypothalamus.
- Blood glucose regulation by insulin and glucagon.
- Blood pressure regulation by the heart, kidneys, and nervous system.
- Blood pH regulation (7.35–7.45) by the lungs and kidneys.

Homeostatic mechanisms use negative feedback loops: a stimulus causes a change, a control centre detects the change, and effectors respond to restore balance.

## Cells

Human cells are eukaryotic — they have a membrane-bound nucleus. Key organelles:
- **Nucleus**: Contains DNA; controls cell activities.
- **Mitochondria**: Produce ATP (energy) through cellular respiration.
- **Ribosomes**: Synthesise proteins.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum**: Processes and transports proteins (rough) and lipids (smooth).
- **Golgi apparatus**: Packages and distributes proteins and lipids.
- **Lysosomes**: Digest cellular waste and pathogens.
- **Cell membrane**: Phospholipid bilayer controlling what enters and exits the cell.

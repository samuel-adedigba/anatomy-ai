# The Skeletal System

## Overview

The skeletal system provides the structural framework of the body, protects vital organs, enables movement, produces blood cells, and stores minerals including calcium and phosphorus.

## Bones

The adult human body contains 206 bones. At birth, infants have around 270 bones; many fuse during childhood and adolescence.

### Functions of Bone

- **Support**: Provides the rigid framework that supports soft tissues and body weight.
- **Protection**: The skull protects the brain; the ribcage protects the heart and lungs; the vertebral column protects the spinal cord.
- **Movement**: Bones act as levers for muscle attachment.
- **Haematopoiesis**: Red and white blood cells are produced in red bone marrow.
- **Mineral storage**: Bones store calcium and phosphate, releasing them into the bloodstream as needed.

### Bone Structure

- **Compact (cortical) bone**: Dense outer layer providing strength.
- **Cancellous (trabecular/spongy) bone**: Porous inner layer containing marrow.
- **Periosteum**: Outer membrane covering bone; contains blood vessels and nerves.
- **Endosteum**: Inner lining of the medullary cavity.

### Types of Bones

- **Long bones**: Arms (humerus, radius, ulna) and legs (femur, tibia, fibula).
- **Short bones**: Wrist (carpals) and ankle (tarsals).
- **Flat bones**: Skull, sternum, scapula, ribs.
- **Irregular bones**: Vertebrae, facial bones.
- **Sesamoid bones**: Patella (kneecap).

## The Spine (Vertebral Column)

The spine consists of 33 vertebrae arranged in five regions:
- **Cervical (C1–C7)**: 7 vertebrae in the neck. C1 (atlas) supports the skull; C2 (axis) allows head rotation.
- **Thoracic (T1–T12)**: 12 vertebrae in the mid-back, each attaching to a pair of ribs.
- **Lumbar (L1–L5)**: 5 large vertebrae in the lower back; bear the most body weight.
- **Sacrum (S1–S5)**: 5 fused vertebrae forming the posterior pelvis.
- **Coccyx**: 4 fused vertebrae forming the tailbone.

The spine has natural curves: cervical and lumbar lordosis (inward); thoracic kyphosis (outward). These curves distribute mechanical stress and maintain balance.

Intervertebral discs sit between vertebrae, acting as shock absorbers. Each disc has a tough outer annulus fibrosus and a soft inner nucleus pulposus. Disc herniation ("slipped disc") occurs when the nucleus bulges through the annulus.

## Joints

A joint is where two or more bones meet. Types include:
- **Synovial joints** (freely movable): Hip, knee, shoulder, elbow. Contain synovial fluid for lubrication.
- **Cartilaginous joints** (limited movement): Intervertebral discs, pubic symphysis.
- **Fibrous joints** (immovable): Skull sutures.

# The Muscular System

## Overview

The muscular system produces movement, maintains posture, stabilises joints, and generates heat. The body contains over 600 named muscles.

## Types of Muscle

- **Skeletal muscle**: Voluntary, striated muscle attached to bones. Controlled consciously.
- **Cardiac muscle**: Involuntary, striated muscle found only in the heart.
- **Smooth muscle**: Involuntary, non-striated muscle in hollow organs (blood vessels, intestines, bladder).

## How Muscles Work

Muscles contract when stimulated by motor neurons. A motor unit consists of a single motor neuron and all the muscle fibres it innervates. Muscle contraction involves sliding filaments: actin and myosin proteins interact when triggered by calcium ions released following an action potential.

Muscles work in pairs:
- **Agonist (prime mover)**: The muscle primarily responsible for a movement (e.g. biceps brachii flexes the elbow).
- **Antagonist**: Opposes the agonist (e.g. triceps brachii extends the elbow).
- **Synergists**: Assist the agonist.
- **Fixators**: Stabilise joints during movement.

## Major Muscle Groups

- **Head and neck**: Masseter (jaw), sternocleidomastoid (head rotation).
- **Chest**: Pectoralis major and minor.
- **Back**: Trapezius, latissimus dorsi, erector spinae (spinal extensors).
- **Abdomen**: Rectus abdominis, external and internal obliques, transverse abdominis.
- **Upper arm**: Biceps brachii (flexion), triceps brachii (extension).
- **Forearm**: Flexors and extensors of the wrist and fingers.
- **Gluteal**: Gluteus maximus (hip extension), gluteus medius and minimus (hip abduction).
- **Thigh**: Quadriceps femoris (knee extension), hamstrings (knee flexion), adductors.
- **Lower leg**: Gastrocnemius and soleus (calf, plantarflexion), tibialis anterior (dorsiflexion).

## Tendons and Ligaments

- **Tendons**: Connect muscle to bone. The Achilles tendon connects the calf muscles to the heel bone.
- **Ligaments**: Connect bone to bone, stabilising joints. The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) stabilises the knee.

## Muscle Fibres

- **Type I (slow-twitch)**: Fatigue-resistant, use oxygen efficiently. Important for endurance activities.
- **Type II (fast-twitch)**: Generate more force rapidly but fatigue faster. Important for power and speed.
